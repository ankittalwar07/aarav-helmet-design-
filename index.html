Why does it harden?

The secret is a shear-thickening fluid (a "non-Newtonian" material). It's full of tiny solid particles floating in liquid.

  • 🐢 Slow push → particles slide past each other → feels like a gel.
  • ⚡ Fast hit → particles jam together into "log-jams" called hydroclusters → feels like a solid.
  • 🔁 It's reversible — once the hit is over, it goes soft again.

📐 The physics of not getting hurt

A helmet's whole job is to make a crash last longer. The same energy spread over more time = a much smaller force on your head.

F = Δp / Δt

Force = change in momentum ÷ time. If the material stretches out the stop (bigger Δt), the force F on the skull drops. That's the magic.

🥣 Prove it at home: make Oobleck

Before building a whole helmet, Aarav can prove the science in 5 minutes in the kitchen. Oobleck is a shear-thickening fluid — exactly the effect his helmet needs.

You need

  • 1 cup cornstarch (corn flour)
  • About ½ cup water
  • A bowl + a spoon
  • Food colouring (optional, fun)

Steps

  1. Add cornstarch to the bowl.
  2. Slowly stir in water until it's like thick honey.
  3. Push slowly → your finger sinks in (soft!).
  4. Punch it fast → it feels hard, splash-free (hard!).
  5. Roll a ball in your hands, then stop — it melts. 🤯

Science-fair questions

  • Does more cornstarch make it harden more easily?
  • Drop a small weight from different heights — does it bounce or sink?
  • Put oobleck in a balloon — does it protect an egg from a drop? 🥚
From kitchen to helmet: Real armor brands (like D3O) use the same idea but with engineered polymers sealed in a soft pad, so it never dries out and stays comfy. Aarav's design = airy shell + a sealed shear-thickening pad + a soft liner that lets his hair breathe.
